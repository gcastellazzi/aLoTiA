## About this example

The arch of Saint-Edme at Nogent-sur-Seine, built by Jean-Rodolphe Perronet in
1768 and published by him in the *Description des projets et de la construction
des ponts de Neuilly, de Mantes, d'Orleans et autres* (Paris, Imprimerie
Royale, 1782), plate XXVIII. The plate shows the same arch twice: on the left
standing on its centering, on the right after decentering, with the voussoirs
numbered 1 to 47 from the springing to the crown.

## Where the geometry comes from

Everything here is read off the plate, and the plate carries its own scale:
the dimension **90 pieds** is engraved across the springings. At 0.3248 m to
the pied du roi that is a span of 29.24 m.

| | |
|---|---|
| span | 29.24 m |
| rise | 8.48 m |
| rise/span | 0.290 |
| ring depth at the crown | 0.85 m |
| voussoirs | 94, as the plate numbers them |

The intrados was traced on the half drawn after decentering -- the arch as it
stands, not as it was built -- and mirrored. **The ring depth is the one
measurable thing on the plate at this resolution**: at the crown the voussoir
heads are drawn, and they are 0.85 m deep. The engraving does not resolve how
the ring deepens towards the springings, so it is held constant, and that is an
assumption rather than a reading.

Weights are per metre of bridge width, at 20 kN/m3.

## What it is here to show

A span entered from a drawing rather than from a photograph, with no
rectification needed and no scale bar to find: the draughtsman put the
dimension on the sheet. It is also the arch whose decentering movements
Perronet measured and drew -- the lines ABC and A'B'C' on the plate -- which is
why the figure is worth tracing rather than redrawing from a formula.
