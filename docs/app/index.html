Circular ring

An exact ring from its numbers, with no image to trace. The published figures for the admissible band and the least admissible thickness are computed on this ring, so a point of either can be checked here by hand.

Trace a whole profile

For a section the two faces cannot describe: a filled haunch, a widening pier, or the two shells of a dome. Trace each closed outline in turn; the joints come from rays out of a picked centre, so every shell is cut at the same angles.

no outline traced

Add a block

Click the corners in turn. Useful for a pier, a fill, or anywhere the radial cut does not suit the shape.

Scale

not scaled — lengths are pixels

Dome slice — Poleni

A barrel vault cut into voussoirs gives blocks of constant width. A dome cut into lunes does not: each is bounded by two meridian planes, so it is broad at the major parallel and closes to nothing at the crown. The weights follow, and so does the line of thrust — this is what Poleni saw in 1748.

off — constant thickness

Points A and B

not set — the ends follow the joints

With both ends imposed the thrust stays free and the other two parameters become results: the pole's ordinate is whatever carries the line from A to B. The force polygon shows the construction — a trial pole, the closing error, the corrected pole.

Applied forces

Click where it acts. Forces are taken as vertical and downward, and join the blocks in the same sequence.

    —

    Three numbers fix the line, and these are they: the pole’s distance from the load line, which is the thrust; where the line leaves its springing joint; and how the total weight divides between the two reactions. Together they are the ∞3 the tool exists to show. The last two need a traced arch, which has joints for the ends to slide along.

    Admissibility

    —

    The thrust line must stay inside the ring. If it does, the safe theorem says the arch stands. A ring too thin at the mid-points of its end joints may still be admissible once those ends are free: the limit line runs through the extrados at both springings.

    Hooke's cable

    A circle at each vertex with its area proportional to the weight carried there. On a dome lune the springing circles dwarf the crown ones, which is the taper made visible.

    Mechanism

    The line of thrust cannot leave the ring: where it would, it stays hooked at the intrados or the extrados between two voussoirs, and that point is a hinge. With this on, the thrust slider alone commands the line — the other two parameters are chosen to keep it as far from both faces as it can be, so hinges form on their own as the thrust is pushed.

    —

    Live only once the arch is a mechanism: the macro-blocks turn about their own instantaneous centres, and the hinges stay shut.
